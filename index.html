STEP 01 · HABIT ONE

Name your delivery moment, write the question, add the number.

Pick the moment you hand over what you built: the end of a session, the results conversation, the walkthrough of a finished job. Ask there, before you leave. Ask two weeks later and you get a verdict. Ask at delivery and you get a reason.

Your delivery moment is the end of a prototype session.
The question · same words every time

One place, filled in before you leave. Not your memory.

People who like you will tell you it was great. Watch what they do: did they come back?

Everything’s fine, and they haven’t booked in five months. That gap is where you learn something. Who’s in it?

STEP 02 · THE WEEKLY READ

Twenty minutes, same day every week. Read the answers.

End every read withWhat are we changing because of this?
The answers that repeat

Tally each answer you hear more than once, in the customer’s words. When the same answer comes from three different customers, it’s earned a test.

From the lesson: the same answer from eleven members by month four, and the two changes Upstream tested, six weeks each, eighteen weeks in all. The lesson doesn’t give calendar dates, so the dates here are set back from today. It also doesn’t name the number watched for the second and third tests, so those are left blank.
STEP 03 · HABIT TWO

One change. One number. One date.

Change three things at once and it’s like fixing a flat sauce with salt, lemon and chili together: it tastes better, and you have no idea which one did it. Put a decision date six weeks out, then leave it alone. On the date, decide out loud.

The trap · the date that slides

The date arrives, the result is unclear, someone says give it a few more weeks, and by the third time the test has become a project nobody will ever decide. Unclear is a result. Write it down, sharpen the question, and start again. Protect the date.
